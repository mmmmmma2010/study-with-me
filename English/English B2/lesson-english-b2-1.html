🎯 Can-do goals

🌱 Warm-up

Think about a moment in your life when one small decision changed everything. Maybe you missed a bus, changed your university major, or said "yes" to a trip on the spur of the moment.

If I had missed that bus,I would never have met my best friend.

This sentence is about the past, but it describes something that did not happen. That is the job of the third conditional.

في العربية نقول: «لو كنتُ فاتني الباص، لما كنتُ قابلتُ أعز أصدقائي». الفكرة نفسها تمامًا: ماضٍ لم يحدث، ونتيجة متخيَّلة.

🎨 Grammar in colour: the third conditional

Look at these three sentences. What do they have in common?

if-clause (condition)had + past participleresultwould/could/might have + past participle
If Ihad knownكنتُ أعرفabout the traffic,Iwould have leftلكنتُ غادرتُearlier.
If shehadn't turned downلو لم ترفضthe offer,shemight have movedربما انتقلتto Dubai.
Wecould have wonكان بإمكاننا الفوزthe matchif wehad takenلو كنا أخذناmore risks.

Did you notice? All three talk about the past, and in all three the condition is not true. I didn't know. She did turn it down. We didn't take risks.

If-clauseResult clauseMeaning
If + had + past participlewould have + past participlecertain imagined result
If + had + past participlecould have + past participleimagined ability / possibility
If + had + past participlemight have + past participleimagined result, less certain
If + hadn't + past participlewouldn't have + past participlenegative forms
💡 Order is flexible. The if-clause can come first or second. Use a comma only when the if-clause comes first.

Which conditional do I need?

The tricky 'd

In speech, both had and would become 'd:

If you'd told me,'d = hadI'd have helped.'d = would
📝 Rule of thumb: 'd + past participle = had. 'd + have = would.

🔁 Arabic vs English

العربية

لو كنتُ قد عرفتُشرطلكنتُ غادرتُ مبكرًاجواب

«لو» + «كان (قد)» + فعل ماضٍ = تركيب قريب جدًا من الإنجليزية.

English

If I had known,I would have left early.

had ≈ كنتُ قد · would have ≈ لـ + كنتُ

✅ Easy for you: التركيب «لو كنتُ قد فعلتُ... لكنتُ فعلتُ» يطابق الإنجليزية تقريبًا. فكّر به عندما تتكلم.
⚠️ Hard for you: في العامية نقول «لو عرفت كان رحت» ونستخدم الماضي البسيط لكل شيء. لذلك يقول كثير من العرب If I knew yesterday ❌ أو يضعون would في جملة الشرط: If I would have known ❌. لا تضع would بعد if أبدًا.

👄 Pronunciation: weak forms & linking

Native speakers almost never say would have in full. have becomes a tiny /əv/ and links to the word before it.

WrittenSpoken (weak form)Listen
would have/ˈwʊdəv/ — "would-uv" I would have called you.
wouldn't have/ˈwʊdntəv/ I wouldn't have gone.
could have/ˈkʊdəv/ We could have won.
might have/ˈmaɪtəv/ She might have missed it.
If I had/ɪˈf‿aɪd/ — linking If I'd known, I'd have stayed.

Sentence stress: stress the main verb and the important words, not would or have:

If I'd KNOWN, I'd have LEFT EARlier.

⚠️ Spelling trap: because would've sounds like "would of", many people write would of ❌. Always write would have or would've ✅.
💡 العربية تنطق كل مقطع بوضوح. في الإنجليزية «ابلع» الكلمات الصغيرة واضغط على الفعل الأساسي.

💬 Real-life dialogue: "The job I turned down"

Lina and Karim, two old university friends, meet at a café after five years. الكلمات المنقّطة: مرّر الفأرة لترى المعنى.

Lina
Karim! I can't believe it's been five years. So, are you still working at the bank in Cairo?
Karim
I am, actually. Do you remember that offer I got from the tech start-up in Berlin? I turned it down.
Lina
Of course I remember. Everyone thought you'd go. Do you ever regret it?
Karim
Honestly? Sometimes. If I had accepted, I would have learned a lot more about software. I might even have started my own company by now.
Lina
So why didn't you go? You're usually the one who takes risks.
Karim
My father was ill that year. If I had moved abroad, I wouldn't have been able to look after him. In hindsight, it was the right call.
Lina
That makes sense. You shouldn't blame yourself. And anyway, the start-up closed two years later, didn't it?
Karim
It did! So if I had gone, I could have ended up unemployed in a foreign country. That would have been a nightmare.
Lina
Sounds like a narrow escape. For me, the turning point was the opposite. I almost didn't apply for my master's. I had second thoughts until the very last day.
Karim
And if you hadn't applied?
Lina
I would have settled for a job I didn't enjoy, and I would never have met the team I work with now. I booked the flight to Toronto on the spur of the moment, and it changed my life.
Karim
Well, I guess we both made the most of the choices we had. Let's not wait another five years, though.
🔎 Comprehension check (open after listening)
  1. Why did Karim turn down the job? — His father was ill and he wanted to look after him.
  2. What happened to the start-up? — It closed two years later.
  3. What would Lina have done if she hadn't applied? — She would have settled for a job she didn't enjoy.

✍️ Writing task (120–180 words)

Write about a turning point in your life. Describe a decision you made (or didn't make). What would have happened if you had chosen differently? Do you regret it, or are you relieved?

  • Use at least three third-conditional sentences.
  • Use could have or might have at least once.
  • Use at least four words from this lesson.

أرسل لي ما تكتبه في المحادثة وسأصححه لك خطوة بخطوة.

👀 Model answer (158 words)

Three years ago, I had to choose between studying engineering in my hometown and accepting a scholarship in Germany. I was terrified of living alone, so I almost turned the scholarship down. In the end, I took the risk, and it became a real turning point in my life.

If I had stayed at home, I would have been more comfortable, but I would never have learned German, and I might not have discovered how independent I can be. I also wouldn't have met my closest friends, who come from five different countries.

Of course, there were difficult moments. If my family hadn't supported me, I could easily have given up during the first winter. In hindsight, though, I have no regrets. I look back on that decision with pride, and I try to make the most of every opportunity that comes my way.

📌 Summary

  • Third conditional = an imagined past: If + had + V3, would have + V3.
  • could have = imagined ability; might have = imagined possibility.
  • Never put would after if.
  • 'd + V3 = had; 'd have = would have.
  • In speech: would've /ˈwʊdəv/ — but never write "would of".
  • فكّر بالتركيب العربي «لو كنتُ قد... لكنتُ...» فهو يطابق الإنجليزية.
